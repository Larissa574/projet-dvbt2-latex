\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Nowadays, the field of information and communication technologies has progressed leading to the appearance of new technologies or services to facilitate our daily lives. Since its discovery, television, like all other communication technologies, has not remained on the sidelines of this evolution. One of the last major changes in the worldwide countries, is the transition from Analog Television to Digital Television. Due to the advantages of digital television, Digital Terrestrial Television (DTT) standards such as European, American, Japanese and Chinese standards have been developed. To help the network broadcasters in the DTT systems deployment, operation and evolution, several studies have been undergone on these standards in \cite{ref1}.

Digital Video Broadcasting-Terrestrial, first generation (DVB-T) and Digital Video Broadcasting-Terrestrial, second generation (DVB-T2) represent the European standard mainly adopted or deployed in Europe and Africa. Among them, DVB-T2 performance are better than the DVB-T one. The main differences between these standards consists in the use of advanced error correcting codes, rotated constellation followed by a cyclic shift and the Multiple Input Signal Output (MISO) technique in the DVB-T2 system \cite{ref2}. Indeed, the rotated constellation technique is a technique first proposed in the DVB-T2 system. It consists in first in the correlation of the I and Q components of a classical constellation, and in second in the transmission of the I and Q components on different sub-carriers. While the forme allows to make unique each constellation symbol, the latter represents the frequency diversity technique which improves DVB-T2 performance in the presence of fading channels \cite{ref3}. However, when the rotated constellation is applied, it induces a demapper complexity due to the taking into account of the channel coefficients at the level of the demodulator \cite{ref4}.

Several values of rotation angles have been studied in the literature as well as the types of channels suitable for these angles. To the best of our knowledge, the works existing in the literature have not proposed an algorithm of the optimal angle detection. Our work consists in studying and determining the optimal angle that allows the reduction of the demapper complexity \cite{ref5}.

The rest of this paper is structured as follows. In the second section, the rotated constellation technique is presented, its advantages as well as its limitations. In the third section, the rotated constellation angles, the general criteria for choosing these angle and the optimal angle detection algorithm are presented.
