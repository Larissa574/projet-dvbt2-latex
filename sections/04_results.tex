\subsection{DVB-T2 system performance}

In this part, DVB-T2 system performance has been evaluated
when only Gaussian noise is the imperfection present on
the signal. As this system is based on Low Density Parity
Check (LDPC) codes, Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM)
and Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM)
techniques, the Bit Error Rate (BER) is the performance
evaluation tool used to highlight the system performance.

To confirm the good behavior of the simulator,
simulations of the native DVB-T2 system performance have
been performed and the obtained results have been compared
to those highlighted in the scientific literature
\cite{ref17} (table~\ref{tab:awgn_standard}).

This is followed by the simulation results using the
optimal angle proposed in this work. The performance
has been evaluated under the AWGN channel with and
without rotated constellation using 16-QAM and 256-QAM
modulations and LDPC code rate 1/2.

The evaluated performances are those giving the bit
error rate (BER) as a function of the signal to noise
ratio (SNR). The results obtained are presented on
(fig.~\ref{fig:ber_rotated}) and
(fig.~\ref{fig:ber_without_rotation}).

\section{Discussion}

The angle values obtained indicate that the proposed
algorithm generates angle values equal to those giving
a rotated constellation when the components are uniformly
spaced. It should be noted that these angle values are
not those adopted by the DVB-T2 standard.

For an M-QAM modulation, these angles are generally
written in the form $\arctan\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}$ and
allow to ensure an optimal distance of the components
of the symbols when the rotated constellation is used,
regardless of the type of channel used.

Indeed a choice of rotation angles depends on several
parameters. However the determination of these angles
by our algorithm depends only on the mapping and the
QAM modulation order. The impact of erasure channels
has not been taken into account in the design of this
algorithm. The DVB-T2 standard has not speculated on
these angle values.

The advantage of using these angles is that they have
interesting properties allowing the development of less
complex 2D de-mapping algorithms when the rotated
constellation and cyclic shift techniques are used
\cite{ref5}.

Such angle values have been studied in various articles.
Our results showed that under AWGN, the performance
obtained with the angles of our algorithm is close to
that specified by the DVB-T2 standard when using the
angles of the standard. This small difference could
be justified by the rather low number of bits
generated during the simulation.

\section{Conclusion}

This work focused on the study of rotated constellation
and the determination of the optimal rotated constellation
angle. In the first part, bibliographical synthesis has
been presented on rotated constellation technique used
in DVB-T2 and proposed for other standards like NOMA
and under-water acoustic communication.

Furthermore, an algorithm of detection of the optimal
rotation angle has been proposed. The obtained angles
have some properties that allow to establish a low
complexity QAM demapping when rotated constellation
is used.

In perspective to our work, it will be a question of
studying the performances of the technique of rotated
constellation with the angles obtained and of studying
the types of compatible channels.
